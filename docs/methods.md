# Methods

This document describes the analytical methods implemented in `imtp-processor` and the rationale for the methodological decisions used by the package.

The methods described here apply to v1.0.0, which uses manual identification of force-onset. Analytical methods may be extended in future releases as additional force-onset detection approaches are implemented.

## Data processing

`imtp-processor` imports force-time data from supported force-plate CSV files and standardises the imported data into a common time and vertical force representation for analysis. Where force is recorded using separate left and right force plates, the vertical force signals are summed to produce the combined vertical ground reaction force used for subsequent analysis.

Analysis procedures are based on elapsed time rather than sample number so that the same time-based procedures can be applied to data collected at different sampling frequencies.

## Bodyweight determination

Bodyweight is determined from a 1 s weighing period selected by the analyst before the initiation of the isometric mid-thigh pull (IMTP). The mean vertical force recorded during this period is used as the estimate of bodyweight for the trial. This value provides the baseline from which net force and the countermovement screening threshold are calculated.

The analyst selects the beginning of the weighing period through visual inspection of the force-time curve. This analyst-in-the-loop allows the weighing period to be positioned within a stable portion of the pre-onset force trace rather than assuming that a predetermined section of every imported file represents quiet standing.

## Countermovement screening

Following determination of bodyweight, the pre-onset force trace is screened for evidence of a countermovement. A trial is flagged when vertical force falls more than 50 N below the calculated bodyweight before force onset. The screening procedure is intended to draw the analyst's attention to trials that may not represent a purely isometric initiation of force production.

The screening result is presented to the analyst as part of the analysis workflow rather than being used to automatically determine force onset.

## Force-onset identification

Version 1.0.0 uses manual force-onset identification. The analyst visually inspects the force-time curve and positions the onset marker at the point judged to represent the initiation of the pull.

Manual identification is retained as an explicitly analyst-controlled step because the method used to identify force onset can materially affect time-dependent IMTP force measurements. Guppy et al. (2024) compared manual identification with automated thresholds of 40 N above bodyweight and 3 and 5 standard deviations above bodyweight and found systematic differences in several resulting force-time characteristics. The methods should therefore not be treated as interchangeable.

The onset selected by the analyst defines time zero for the subsequent calculation of the force-time variables.

## Force-time variables

Force is expressed as net force relative to the bodyweight determined during the weighing period.

**Net force = vertical ground reaction force - bodyweight**

`imtp-processor` reports force at:

- 50 ms after force onset;
- 100 ms after force onset;
- 150 ms after force onset;
- 200 ms after force onset;
- 250 ms after force onset;

Peak net force is also extracted from the analysed force-time curve.

Force at a specified time after onset divided by the elapsed time is numerically equivalent to the average rate of force development (RFD) over the interval from force onset to that time:

**Average RFD = net force at time / t**

For example, net force at 250 ms can therefore be used to calculate average RFD from 0-250 ms by dividing the corresponding force value by 0.250 s.

The use of time-specific force characteristics and the implementation of force-time analysis in Python are further discussed by Smith et al. (2025).

## Interpretation and reproducibility

The analytical choices used to process force-time curve data form part of the measurement procedure and should be reported when results generated by `imtp-processor` are used in research or practice. In particular, force-onset methodology should be considered when comparing time-dependent force characteristics between analyses. Results obtained using different onset-identification methods should not automatically be considered interchangeable (Guppy et al., 2024).

To support reproducibility, users should report the version of `imtp-processor` that is used for analysis along with the relevant analytical procedures.
